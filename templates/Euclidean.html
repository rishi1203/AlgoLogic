Euclidean Algorithm (GCD) Animation

What is Euclidean:

The Euclidean algorithm is an efficient method for finding the greatest common divisor (GCD) of two integers. It's based on the principle that the GCD of two numbers remains the same if the larger number is replaced by its difference with the smaller number.

Operation of Euclidean:

  • Initialization: Start with two positive integers, a and b, where a is greater than or equal to b.
  • Division: Divide a by b and compute the remainder r.
  • Update: Replace a with b and b with r.
  • Repeat: Repeat steps 2-3 until the remainder r is zero. The GCD is the value of b when r becomes zero.

    • Application of Euclidean:

  • Finding Coprime Numbers: The Euclidean algorithm can determine whether two numbers are coprime by checking if their GCD is 1.
  • Modular Arithmetic: It's used in modular arithmetic to find the modular multiplicative inverse.

    • Advantages of Euclidean:

  • Efficiency: The Euclidean algorithm is very efficient and has a time complexity of O(log min(a, b)), where a and b are the two integers.
  • Simplicity: It's straightforward to implement and understand, making it widely used in various applications involving integer arithmetic.

    • Disadvantages of Euclidean:

  • Limited to Integers: The Euclidean algorithm is specifically designed for finding the GCD of integers and cannot be directly applied to other types of numbers, such as real numbers or complex numbers.
  • Potential for Large Numbers: While the algorithm is efficient for most cases, it may become less efficient for extremely large numbers due to the repeated division operations. In such cases, more advanced algorithms may be required.
